When Apple introduced Liquid Glass, the keynote and the marketing images showed something new:
controls made of thick, clear glass. The content underneath bent around their edges, light split
into thin rainbows at the rim, and every button looked like something you could pick up off the
screen.

What ended up on our phones is a quieter thing. To me most of it reads as a blur with a bright
outline, and over the betas it got frostier, mostly so that text on top stays readable. That's a
reasonable trade for an operating system millions of people use all day. But the look from the
keynote never really arrived.

So I built a place where it does. **Liquid Glass Lab** renders real refractive glass in the
browser: every surface has a thickness and a shape, and the page behind it is bent through it, as
real glass would. You can drag it around, tune every property of the glass, and share what you
made with a link.

[Open Liquid Glass Lab](/liquid-glass-lab/)

## Blur isn't glass

Most "glass" on the web, and much of it in today's interfaces, is a blur: the content behind is
smeared and dimmed, and a light border suggests an edge. Glass with thickness does something else
entirely. It acts as a lens: straight lines bend where the rim curves, the content just outside
gets pulled into the edge, and the middle stays sharp.

## Glass that behaves like a liquid

Two pieces of glass that come close don't just overlap: they merge, with a soft neck between them,
like drops of water on a table. The **Group** shape takes this further: nine drops held together,
so pulling one sends a ripple through the rest and stretches the necks before they settle back.

<Shots>
  <Shot video src="/meet-liquid-glass-lab/group-drag.mp4" poster="/meet-liquid-glass-lab/group-drag.webp" alt="A group of glass drops joined by liquid necks, dragged over coloured lines, rippling and stretching before it settles" caption="The Group shape: drag one drop and the rest follow." />

Drag any shape and it tips in perspective, like a stone pulled across a table, and settles when
you let go.

## Every property of real glass

The panel on the right holds everything that makes glass look like glass, from its shape to its
optics and light:

- **Form:** thickness, how wide and how round the rim is, a raised edge or a flat cap, a dome that
  magnifies, and how eagerly two drops merge.
- **Optics:** how strongly it bends light, how much it splits colours, and how frosted it is.
- **Light:** where the light comes from, highlights, reflections, a glowing rim and iridescence.
- **Surface:** tint, a shadow, and the bright caustic that glass throws onto the page below.

Together, these are enough to build any liquid glass look: the bold one from the keynote, the
calm one that shipped, and plenty that nobody has shipped yet.

At the top of the panel, a **side view** shows a cut through the glass at true scale, with lines
of sight showing where each point of the glass takes its picture from. Move a slider and you see
the shape change before you see the result.

## Share what you make

Every look fits in a short code at the end of the link: about seven characters for a preset, a
few more for your own tweaks. Press
**Copy link**, send it, and whoever opens it gets exactly your glass, on your shape and backdrop.

## How it works

The lab is a single web page with no build step, rendered with WebGL2. The glass shape is a
smooth distance field, turned into a height map with a rim and an optional dome. For every pixel,
a line of sight is bent through that surface with the same law that describes real refraction,
then traced to where it lands on the backdrop. Sampling it at seven slightly different strengths
gives the rainbow fringes; reflections, highlights, a shadow and the caustic are added on top.

It runs in any current browser, on a phone too, though a bigger screen shows it off best. Like
most things I build lately, it was made together with Claude.
